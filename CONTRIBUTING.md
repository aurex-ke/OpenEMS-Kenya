# Contributing

Thank you for your interest in the Open-Source EMS Pilot Programme Kenya
(OpenEMS Kenya). Contributions that improve the research, documentation, and
eventual deployment resources are welcome.

## Ways to contribute

- Correct errors or clarify existing documents.
- Suggest improvements to the research design, feasibility assumptions, or
  evaluation methods.
- Add evidence, references, or relevant Kenya-specific context.
- Improve spreadsheets, configuration examples, or software as these are
  prepared for release.
- Report unclear, missing, or outdated information.

For substantial changes, open an issue or contact the maintainers before
starting work. This helps establish the scope and avoid duplicating effort.

## Making a change

1. Check existing issues and documents to see whether the work is already
   underway.
2. Describe the problem and proposed change in an issue, especially for
   significant or cross-document work.
3. Start from the latest `develop` branch. Create a focused working branch
   from it (or use a fork if you do not have permission to create branches in
   this repository). Do not push changes directly to `main`.
4. Make a focused change. Keep unrelated edits in separate contributions.
5. Open a pull request targeting `develop`, with a concise summary, the reason
   for the change, and any relevant issue links.
6. Respond to review feedback and update the pull request as needed.

Changes are reviewed and integrated into `develop` before maintainers promote
them to `main`.

## Documentation and research standards

- Write clearly and use respectful, inclusive language.
- Prefer precise claims supported by credible, linked sources. Include the
  source's publication date and access date when useful.
- Distinguish measured results, sourced facts, estimates, assumptions, and
  recommendations. Do not present proposed targets or international findings
  as verified Kenya pilot outcomes.
- For figures, state the currency, units, time period, and relevant
  assumptions. Identify estimates and explain material changes to calculations.
- Keep terminology, units, and names consistent across documents. Update
  related documents when a change affects them.
- Use descriptive filenames and preserve the existing organization under
  `docs/`.
- For spreadsheet changes, preserve formulas where possible and explain changes
  to assumptions, units, or methodology in the workbook or accompanying
  documentation.

## Data, privacy, and safety

Do not submit personal, confidential, commercially sensitive, or otherwise
restricted information. Do not publish site-level operational data, credentials,
or security-sensitive details unless the project has confirmed that they are
approved for public release. Use anonymized or synthetic examples when possible.

Flag potential privacy, cybersecurity, electrical-safety, or regulatory issues
in the relevant issue or pull request. Do not include live credentials, access
tokens, private keys, or other secrets.

## Review and acceptance

Contributions may be reviewed for accuracy, evidence, clarity, consistency with
project scope, and suitability for public release. A contribution may require
revision or may not be accepted if its claims cannot be substantiated or it
includes information that should not be public.
