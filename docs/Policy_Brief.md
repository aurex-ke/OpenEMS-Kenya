# Policy Brief

## 1. Purpose and bottom line

The README assigns E4 to the Legal and regulatory lead, for the Ministry of Energy and EPRA: recommendations on net-metering, battery storage (BESS) and EV charging rules, drawn from the pilot. The Charter is explicit that the programme **informs** tariffs and regulation but does **not decide** them (Charter Section 5). This document therefore does two things. Part A fixes how evidence becomes a policy recommendation. Part B is the brief itself, with each section pre-structured and its findings left blank until the pilot has produced them.

**Status note.** No site is selected, built or measured, and no evidence exists. This version is a **pre-populated template**. It must not be used to state or imply a result, or to suggest that any regulator has endorsed the pilot (MoU Section 12).

**Bottom line.** Eight rules govern the brief:

1. **Evidence first.** Every recommendation cites a finding in E2, a monthly report (D6) or the regulatory log, and carries an evidence tag.
2. **Inform, do not pre-empt.** The brief offers options and evidence. It does not claim pre-approval of any licence, tariff or interconnection (MoU Section 5.2).
3. **Per scenario, not national.** Three sites support three scenario findings. No national claim is made (E2 Section 9.4).
4. **Conditions before claims.** Each recommendation states the tariff, size, topology and hardware conditions under which it holds.
5. **Negative findings count.** A result below the 15 to 30% range, or a rule that created friction, is reported with equal prominence (Charter Section 21).
6. **Independence.** The Research Lead's findings are not edited by regulators, hosts, vendors or funders. They may comment on drafts for [14] days and may request redaction of personal or confidential data (MoU Section 10).
7. **Costs and trade-offs shown.** Where a change helps one group (for example hosts) the brief shows effects on others (for example the utility's revenue and network).
8. **Short and usable.** A two-page decision summary for officials, and a longer evidence annex for technical staff.

## 4. Policy questions the brief addresses

Each question below is linked to evidence the pilot is designed to produce. The "what the pilot can show" column is deliberately limited.

| # | Policy question | Link to pilot | What the pilot can show | What it cannot show |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | **Net-metering design.** Does the 50% export credit (Net-Metering Regulations 2024) steer hosts toward efficient storage and self-consumption, and how sensitive is viability to the credit level? | Pilot A (K2, K3), B2 sensitivity runs at and below 50% | Measured self-consumption and bill effect under the confirmed terms; viability at lower and higher credits as a modelled sensitivity | The effect on the wider system or on Kenya Power's revenue |
| P2 | **Treatment of storage added to existing net-metered PV.** Does it need a new or amended application, and how is stored-then-exported energy treated? | B5 Section 5.1, Pilot A | Time, steps and outcome experienced by one host | A general rule for all categories |
| P3 | **Licensing of behind-the-meter BESS** at about 200 to 250 kWh: licence, permit, notification or exemption? | B5 Section 5.2, all pilots | The position EPRA gives, lead time, conditions of any exemption or sandbox | Whether the same position holds for larger systems |
| P4 | **Mini-grid and small supply arrangements.** Which operator, tariff and consumer-protection rules apply to a solar-plus-storage system serving a village or estate? | Pilot B, B5 Section 5.3 | How the arrangement worked, complaints, supply continuity, regulatory friction | Compliance of other operators |
| P5 | **EV charging station rules.** Is licensing or registration needed, and what pricing, display, safety and managed-charging disclosure rules are appropriate? | Pilot C, B5 Section 5.4 | Operator experience, driver reaction to throttling, wait-time distribution, transformer loading | Network-wide EV effects |
| P6 | **Interconnection and utility data.** Are protection, anti-islanding, metering and data-access requirements workable for customer-side storage and charger management? | Kenya Power MoU, B5 Section 5.5 | Time to approval, conditions, data-feed response time, control boundary (behind the connection point only) | Distribution planning at scale |
| P7 | **Managed charging as a connection alternative.** Could demonstrated site-limit control support connection of chargers to constrained transformers? | Pilot C, K5 | Whether load management held the agreed limit without unacceptable delay | Whether to change utility connection standards, which is Kenya Power's decision |
| P8 | **Standards and certification** for batteries, inverters and chargers (safety, grid code, OCPP security profile) | B6, C6, B7 | Which standards the insurer, Kenya Power and the compatibility test actually required | A national standard set |
| P9 | **Data protection and cybersecurity** expectations for connected energy assets | D2, B7 | Experience with registration, DPIA, pseudonymisation, audit findings | Sector rules that do not yet exist |
| P10 | **Open-source and procurement.** Do public-funded procurement and licensing rules accommodate open-source control software and written warranty permission for third-party control? | C1, B5 Sections 5.10 and 5.11 | Tender experience, vendor resistance, licence compliance record | Public procurement law outcomes beyond the pilot entity |

## 5. Evidence rules for recommendations

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Every recommendation links to a lesson ID (E3), an E2 finding, a regulatory log entry or a test record |
| Strength | Rated: **Strong** (consistent across sites or months, Panel-validated), **Moderate** (one site, validated), **Weak** (single observation or unvalidated), **None** (hypothesis only) |
| Tag | [H], [P], [C], [N] as defined at the top of this brief. At v1.0 no [H] may support a recommendation to change a rule |
| Condition | Tariff, size, topology, device class, season and regulatory snapshot date are stated |
| Sensitivity | For economic claims, the Charter sensitivities (battery cost ±20%, discount rate 5 to 12%, export credit below 50%, tariff spread) are shown |
| Snapshot | Rules are described as at the pilot period. A later change may alter the economics (E2 Section 15) |
| Distribution | Effects on hosts, other customers, the utility and the public are stated where evidence allows. Where not, the brief says so |
| No overreach | Where the pilot cannot support a rule change, the brief says "evidence insufficient" and describes what additional evidence would be needed |

### 6.3 Options and recommendations

Recommendations are separated from findings and framed as options with trade-offs. Option headings are illustrative, **not conclusions**. Each is kept, changed or removed according to the evidence.

| Policy area | Option types to be assessed | Evidence needed to support each |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net-metering and storage | (a) Keep current terms and issue clarifying guidance on storage added to existing systems; (b) adjust the export credit for storage-backed systems; (c) introduce time-differentiated export credits | K2 and K3 under the confirmed terms; model at and below 50% and above; effect on the utility if known |
| BESS licensing | (a) Confirm an exemption or notification route below a size threshold; (b) require a permit with standard conditions (fire, insurer, end of life); (c) make no change | EPRA experience, lead times, safety and insurer findings, incident record |
| Mini-grid and small supply | (a) Clarify the operator and tariff route for small customer-side systems on existing feeders; (b) provide a simplified pathway for cooperatives and estates; (c) no change | Pilot B outcomes, supply continuity, consumer protection, complaints |
| EV charging stations | (a) Registration with managed-charging disclosure rules (policy, maximum wait, signage); (b) recognise load management as a connection option subject to Kenya Power agreement; (c) no change | K5 results, wait distribution, driver feedback, transformer data experience |
| Interconnection and data | (a) Standard checklist and timeline for customer-side storage; (b) a read-only data-sharing template for feeders and transformers; (c) approved equipment list | Kenya Power timelines and conditions, data response time, control boundary compliance |
| Standards and certification | (a) Name accepted battery and charger safety standards and an OCPP security profile; (b) require written third-party control permission in public tenders | B6 results, insurer requirements, vendor refusals |
| Data protection and cybersecurity | (a) Guidance on controller and processor roles for energy data; (b) a minimum security baseline for remotely connected storage and chargers | D2 and B7 experience, audit findings, incident record |
| Open source and procurement | (a) Confirm that open-source control layers are acceptable in public tenders; (b) support upstream contribution of drivers | C1 experience, licence compliance record |
| Capacity and skills | (a) Recognise programme-style operator competence in licensing or training frameworks; (b) fund local trainers | D4 record, retention, assessment results |

### 6.5 Limits of the evidence

| Limitation | Effect on the brief |
| --- | --- |
| Three sites, one per scenario | No statistical generalisation to the national fleet |
| Twelve-month operating window | One cycle of seasons and tariffs. No multi-year degradation or policy-change effect observed |
| Regulatory snapshot | Findings reflect the rules and EPRA positions in force during the pilot |
| No system-level data | The pilot cannot estimate effects on national peak, losses or utility revenue |
| Equipment and firmware specific | Compatibility findings apply to the tested device set |
| Host behaviour and benefit sharing | Possible confounding, recorded in D5 |
