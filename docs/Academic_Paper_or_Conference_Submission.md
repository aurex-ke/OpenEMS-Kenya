# Academic Paper or Conference Submission

## 1. Purpose and bottom line

The README assigns E6 to the Research Lead, for the academic community: peer-reviewed evidence. The Charter commits the programme to open publication of methods, data and findings (Sections 12 and 21) and to independent evaluation (Section 23). The Final Evaluation Report (E2) answers the central question for funders and regulators. This document (E6) defines **how the same evidence is turned into a peer-reviewed paper or conference submission**: what is claimed, what is not, which venue types fit, who is an author, how independence and open publication are protected through peer review, and what the manuscript contains. It also holds the manuscript skeleton, claims register, checklists and templates.

**Status note.** No site is selected, built or measured, and no result exists. This version is therefore a **pre-populated manuscript plan and template**. The structure, claim rules, authorship rules, venue strategy, review chain and templates are fixed now. Every result, number and conclusion field is completed in Months 21 to 24 from the Panel-validated record (D6), the interim evaluation (E1) and the final evaluation (E2). It must not be used to state or imply a result, or to suggest that any journal, conference, regulator or host has endorsed the pilot (MoU Section 12).

**Bottom line.** A paper is read by people who were not in the room, so its credibility rests on pre-registration, uncertainty and honest limits. Ten rules govern it:

1. **The paper reports E2, it does not outrun it.** Every quantitative claim traces to a Panel-validated figure in E2 or D6. A claim in the paper that E2 does not support is removed, not softened (Section 7).
2. **Pre-registered analyses are reported in full.** All pre-registered KPIs (K1 to K6) and the verdict rules fixed at Gate 2 appear in the paper. Added analyses are labelled exploratory (D1 Section 12.1, E2 Section 1).
3. **Per site, not national.** Three heterogeneous sites support three scenario findings. The paper makes no national or statistical-generalisation claim (E2 Section 9.4, Policy Brief Section 1).
4. **The 15 to 30% range is a hypothesis.** The paper frames it as an international reference tested locally, with the site result and its interval, never as a target the programme met or missed (Problem Statement Section 3).
5. **Negative and null results are published as findings.** A result below the range, a failed compatibility test, or a "not viable" verdict is a research result, not a failure of the programme (Charter Section 16).
6. **Independence survives peer review.** Hosts, vendors, funders and regulators may correct facts and request redaction of personal or confidential data. They cannot require changes to evidenced findings (MoU Section 10.2). The same applies to reviewer-driven changes: methods and verdict rules are not altered after data are seen to satisfy a reviewer.
7. **Open and reproducible.** The paper cites the open release (E5) by version and persistent identifier, and states plainly what could not be released and why (Section 9).
8. **Privacy and security first.** Nothing in the manuscript, figures, supplements or reviewer files breaks the disclosure rules in D2 Section 10 or the withheld list in C4 Section 14.
9. **Authorship follows contribution.** Credit follows recognised contribution criteria and the MoU Schedule 1C. Vendors do not analyse their own devices and are not authors of evaluation analyses (Section 8).
10. **Local ownership.** The programme aims for Kenyan co-authorship, including local operators, technicians and early-career researchers where they made qualifying contributions, and for an open-access route Kenyan readers can reach (Section 10).

**What cannot be settled from desk research.** The results, the verdict, the venue, the submission date, the author list and the data release tier are not known. This document fixes the method and leaves those as placeholders for Gates 1 to 5.

## 4. Principles

| Principle | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Claim only what the record supports | Each claim has a source in E2 or D6, an evidence strength and a stated condition |
| Method before result | Methods are described as pre-registered, with any deviation (E2 Section 4.4) disclosed in the paper |
| Uncertainty with every number | Intervals, completeness and mode records accompany every reported effect |
| Mechanism before magnitude | A saving is credited to OpenEMS only where the mechanism is visible in the data (D1 Section 12.1) |
| Conditions before claims | Tariff, size, topology, device and firmware set, season and regulatory snapshot date are stated |
| Honest about limits | Three sites, a twelve-month window, counterfactual model error, Pilot B loss separation and Pilot C demand reconstruction are limits stated in the abstract or main text, not buried |
| Reproducible within disclosure limits | Code, configuration and anonymised data are released so a reader can regenerate published figures, within D2 limits |
| Proportionate | The paper is a paper. Governance detail that readers do not need stays in E2 and the programme documents |
| Respect for participants | Hosts, communities and drivers are described in aggregate and by role, with consent where identifiable |
| No overreach on policy | Findings may be described as relevant to policy. Rule changes are for the regulator (Charter Section 5) |

### 5.2 Why a protocol output is worth considering

Peer reviewers and readers give more weight to analyses fixed before data are seen. E6-B turns the Gate 2 pre-registration into a public, time-stamped record, which protects the programme against later claims that the verdict rules were adjusted (E2 risk FE3). It costs little if prepared from the M&V protocol. The decision is taken at Gate 1 and does not delay the evaluation.

## 6. Manuscript structure and content rules

The skeleton below follows the usual structure of an empirical energy-systems paper. Content is drafted from the sources shown. Bracketed fields are completed from the record.

### 6.1 Title, abstract and keywords

| Element | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Names the technology, the setting, the design and, where the venue allows, the result type ("pilot", "three-site"). No promotional wording |
| Abstract | Structured: background, objective, methods, results, conclusions, limitations. States the sites, duration, data window, and the main per-site results with intervals. States the verdict per scenario. States that results apply to the tested set. Template in Appendix A |
| Keywords | Energy management system, OpenEMS, battery storage, EV charging, Kenya, open source, [others] |
| Plain-language summary | Where the venue allows, in English and Kiswahili, with no promise of savings, jobs or tariffs |

### 6.2 Introduction

**Content.** The Kenyan context (renewable share, peak demand, losses, EV growth, ToU and the 50% net-metering credit), the problem of local coordination of storage, solar, loads and chargers, and the gap in documented OpenEMS performance in Africa. State the central question and the supporting questions Q1 to Q5. Describe what the paper adds and what it does not.

**Sources.** Problem Statement Sections 1 to 4, Executive Summary Sections 2 to 4.

**Rules.**

- Kenyan statistics are reported as in the programme documents (for example, over 80% renewable generation, 23.4% losses against a 17.5% benchmark, peak 2,316 MW in February 2025) and are **re-verified against the primary sources at the time of writing**, with the retrieval date stated.
- The Research Report contains source references in a form that cannot be taken as verified. Each is checked against the original publication before it is cited. A reference that cannot be located is dropped.
- International evidence, including the 15 to 30% savings range, is cited to primary studies, described as a range from other settings and as a hypothesis for Kenya.

### 6.3 Background and related work

**Content.** OpenEMS architecture, controllers and interfaces; evidence on battery dispatch for peak shaving and ToU arbitrage; evidence on solar-plus-storage in mini-grids and on loss reduction; evidence on managed EV charging and transformer loading; open-source energy management and deployment experience in low and middle income settings.

**Rules.** A balanced review that includes studies reporting small or negative effects. No reliance on vendor marketing. Where evidence is thin (for example, documented OpenEMS deployments in Africa), the paper says so. Claims about OpenEMS features cite the documentation for the pinned release used in the pilot, with the version stated.

### 6.4 Study design and methods

| Subsection | Content | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design | Within-site, baseline-versus-controlled comparison with a weather-, load- and tariff-adjusted counterfactual. No control site. Three heterogeneous sites reported separately | D1 Section 3.2 |
| Pre-registration | What was fixed before data (KPIs, analysis plan, verdict rules, thresholds), when, and by whom. Citation to E6-B or the registered protocol if published | D1, E2 Section 4.4 |
| Sites and configurations | Pilot A, B, C as built, with sizes as confirmed by the B4 sizing method (not the Charter reference sizes). Topology at Pilot B. Charger models and OCPP versions at Pilot C. Site selection process and any substitution | E2 Section 5.3, B4, B6 |
| Platform and versions | OpenEMS release, Edge image, driver set, device firmware (version-pinned), configuration tag and hash | C4, B6 |
| Control strategy | Controllers enabled, enablement order, safe states and limits (non-sensitive values only) | B4, C3, C4 |
| Measurement | Measurement points, resolution, independent research meters and logger, time synchronisation, meter accuracy checks | D1 Section 8 |
| Operating modes and schedule | M0 to M4, mode schedule, control-off weeks if used, baseline length | D1 Section 7, C4 Section 6.6 |
| KPIs | K1 to K6 definitions and exclusions (dictionary in supplement) | D1 Appendix A |
| Counterfactual models | Model form per site, fitting data, fit criterion, out-of-sample error | D1 Section 7.4 |
| Uncertainty | Combined uncertainty method (meter, sampling, model, missing data) | D1 Section 8.3 |
| Economic analysis | Net benefit, payback, NPV, cost per kW of peak reduced, levelised cost of stored energy; **pilot cost and replication cost reported separately**; sensitivities (battery cost ±20%, discount rate 5 to 12%, export credit below 50%, tariff spread, inflation about 5%) | Feasibility Study 5.4, E2 Section 9.1 |
| Verdict rules | Lens ratings, thresholds, outcome table, fixed at Gate 2 | E2 Section 9 |
| Ethics and approvals | NACOSTI licence, institutional ethics clearance, data protection registration, consent and notice at Pilots B and C | B5 5.9, D2 |
| Data quality | Completeness, reconciliation, restatements, exclusions | E2 Section 4.3 |
| Deviations | Any deviation from the pre-registered plan, with reason, approver and effect | E2 Section 4.4 |

**Rules.** Methods describe what was done, in the past tense, including what went wrong. They do not describe the plan where practice differed. Security-sensitive detail (network design, credentials, unpatched issues) is excluded (Section 9).

### 6.5 Results

Results follow the E2 order and use the same KPI blocks. Each result is reported with its interval, completeness, mode record and category (D1 Section 12.1).

| Subsection | Content | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery and data | Sites actually evaluated, months of validated controlled data per site, data quality summary, restatements | E2 4, 5 |
| Operating record | Uptime, incidents by priority, changes, safe-state events, drift, failed or rolled-back changes (sanitised) | E2 5.4, D5 |
| Pilot A | K1, K2, K3 with intervals; mechanism split (peak or demand charge, ToU shifting, export credit); tariff reality; seasonality; host effects | E2 6.2 |
| Pilot B | K6 total loss change, and separation only if defensible; K2 and diesel offset; reliability; supply continuity; data gaps; metering coverage | E2 6.3 |
| Pilot C | K5a, K5b, wait-time distribution, fairness, acceptance; limits of claim | E2 6.4 |
| Comparison with the range | K3 per site as % of counterfactual bill, interval, category, position against 15 to 30% | E2 Section 7 |
| Diagnosis | For any result below target or below the range, the fixed diagnosis order and the finding | E2 Section 8 |
| Techno-economics | Net benefit, payback, NPV on replication cost, break-even conditions per site, sensitivities | E2 9.1 |
| Verdict | Lens ratings and the per-scenario answer | E2 9.3, 9.4 |
| Institutional and capacity findings | Regulatory positions obtained, interconnection experience, operators trained, competent and retained | E2 Section 10, D4 |

**Reporting rules.**

1. Every table and figure states the site, window, mode, unit and uncertainty.
2. A KPI that was "not reportable" is shown as not reportable with the reason. A blank is never used (D6 Section 11.1).
3. No result from provisional or unvalidated months appears.
4. Sites are not averaged into a single estimate. Pooled figures, if shown, are labelled descriptive and carry no inferential claim.
5. Figures show distributions and intervals, not only point estimates, and do not rely on colour alone.
6. Pilot C and Pilot B figures respect the disclosure thresholds (minimum group size k, resolution limits) in D2 Section 10.

### 6.6 Discussion

**Content.** The answer to the central question per scenario; where the evidence agrees or disagrees with the international range and with earlier studies; mechanisms; the conditions under which the result holds; the cost of "free" software (integration, skills, service); regulatory and institutional lessons relevant to replication; implications for utilities, operators and developers.

**Rules.**

- The discussion says what the findings suggest, and distinguishes that from what they show.
- It restates the verdict as pre-registered, without reframing a null or negative result as a partial success.
- Policy implications are described as considerations and are consistent with E4. The programme informs regulation and does not decide it (Charter Section 5).
- Alternative explanations and confounders (host behaviour, benefit sharing, weather, tariff changes) are discussed using the confounder log (D5).

### 6.7 Limitations

State, at minimum: three sites, one per scenario, so no statistical generalisation; one twelve-month cycle, so no multi-year degradation or tariff-change effects; counterfactual model error carried into intervals; host behaviour and benefit sharing; Pilot B loss separation; Pilot C demand reconstruction; results specific to the tested equipment, firmware and OpenEMS release; regulatory snapshot date (E2 Section 15).

### 6.8 Conclusions

Short. Per scenario. No new results. No promises. A final sentence on what evidence would change the conclusion and what the next test should be.

## 7. Claims register and evidence rules

Every substantive claim in the manuscript has a row. The register is maintained by the Research Lead and checked by the Panel before submission, after revision and after acceptance.

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Each claim cites an E2 section, a D6 report ID or a validated dataset ID |
| Strength | **Strong** (consistent across months or sites, Panel-validated), **Moderate** (one site, validated), **Weak** (single observation or unvalidated), **None** (hypothesis only). Only Strong and Moderate claims appear as findings. Weak claims appear, if at all, as exploratory observations |
| Tag | [H], [P], [C], [N] as in E3 Section 6.7. No [H] supports a result claim |
| Condition | Tariff, size, topology, device and firmware set, season, regulatory snapshot date |
| Language | "Measured", "observed", "estimated" are used precisely. "Prove", "guarantee", "will save" are not used |
| Causal claims | Only where the mechanism is visible in the data and the diagnosis order has been applied (E2 Section 8) |
| Comparisons | Against the counterfactual or baseline, never against an unstated before-and-after |
| Extrapolation | Annual or multi-year figures from partial data are labelled estimates with the approved seasonal adjustment |
| Consistency | Numbers match E2 exactly. A difference needs a stated reason (for example, later restatement) |

Template for the register in Appendix B.

### 7.1 Handling differences between E2 and the paper

If peer review, a new analysis or a restatement would make the paper differ from E2, the Research Lead records the difference, the Panel advises, and E2 is corrected by a dated addendum (Charter Section 20) so the published record stays consistent. The paper does not become the only place where a number changed.

| Requests to soften or remove negative findings | Declined where the finding is evidenced. Wording can be improved for clarity |
| Requests that would breach disclosure rules | Declined. Alternative aggregated evidence is offered |
| Response to reviewers | Point-by-point, polite, evidence-based, with line references. Template in Appendix E |
| Involved parties | Substantive revisions that affect findings about hosts, Kenya Power, EPRA or vendors are shared with them before resubmission, within the [14]-day comment window, and without giving them control |
| Rejection | The paper is revised and submitted to the next venue on the shortlist. The preprint and E2 remain public. A rejection is logged and the reasons reviewed for lessons |
| Version control | Each version is tagged and stored with the claims register and the response letters |
