# MoU and Site Agreement Templates

## How to use this pack

| Part | Instrument | Binding? | Needed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Master MoU terms, used for three MoUs: (1) Ministry of Energy and EPRA, (2) Kenya Power, (3) University partner | Mostly non-binding, with binding clauses on confidentiality, data, IP, publication and law | Gate 0 to Gate 1 |
| B | Host Site Agreement, used for Pilots A, B and C with a site schedule for each | Binding | Before Gate 1 approval; signed before procurement (Gate 2) |
| C | Signature and drafting checklist | n/a | All gates |

Why two layers: MoUs record cooperation, roles and expectations among institutions that cannot commit funds or regulatory decisions in advance. Site agreements are enforceable contracts covering access, equipment ownership, safety, data and liability, which the Charter requires before procurement (Section 17).

# Part A: Master Memorandum of Understanding

**MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING**

**between**

**\[Programme Lead Entity / Sponsor\]** (the "**Programme Lead**")

**and**

**\[Partner: Ministry of Energy and Petroleum and the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) / Kenya Power and Lighting Company PLC / University name\]** (the "**Partner**")

**on the Open-Source EMS Pilot Programme Kenya (the "Programme")**

Dated \[date\]

## 1. Background

1.1 Kenya generated over 80% of its electricity from renewables in FY2024/25, while system losses averaged 23.4% against a 17.5% regulatory benchmark, peak demand reached 2,316 MW in February 2025, and clustered EV charging is growing quickly.

1.2 The Programme will test, through a 24-month, three-site pilot, whether the open-source energy management system OpenEMS can stabilise a high-renewable grid, reduce losses, lower energy costs and safely integrate EV charging, and whether this is technically, economically and institutionally viable in Kenya.

1.3 The parties wish to record how they will cooperate. Terms used here follow the Programme Charter and Governance Plan (the "**Charter**").

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this MoU is to set out the roles, contributions and working arrangements of the parties for the Programme, as detailed in the Partner Schedule (Schedule 1).

## 3. Guiding principles

The parties will act in line with the Charter principles: evidence first; open by default; regulator involvement from the start; safety and security before speed; local capacity as a deliverable; and proportionate control.

## 4. Roles and contributions

4.1 Each party will perform the roles in Schedule 1 in good faith and within its legal mandate.

4.2 The Partner will nominate a **senior representative** and an **alternate** to the Programme Steering Committee or the relevant Programme body named in Schedule 1, and a **focal point** for day-to-day coordination, within \[14\] days of signature.

4.3 The Programme Lead will keep the Partner informed through the reporting cadence in the Charter (quarterly Steering Committee, twice-yearly Regulatory and Stakeholder Forum, and the monthly dashboard on request).

## 5. Governance and decisions

5.1 Decisions on scope, budget and stage gates sit with the Programme Steering Committee under the Charter. Nothing in this MoU transfers those powers.

5.2 The Partner's participation does not fetter any statutory, regulatory or licensing function. Where the Partner is a regulator or public authority, the Programme will not be treated as pre-approval of any application, licence, tariff or interconnection. Each such matter follows its normal process.

5.3 The parties will raise concerns early through the Programme Manager. Unresolved matters escalate to the Steering Committee Chair.

## 6. Funding

6.1 This MoU does not commit either party to provide funds unless Schedule 1 says so. Any financial commitment will be made by a separate written funding agreement.

6.2 Each party bears its own costs of participation, unless agreed otherwise in writing.

## 7. Data and data protection (binding)

7.1 The parties will process personal data only as needed for the Programme and in line with the **Data Protection Act, 2019** and its regulations.

7.2 Where one party shares personal data with another, the parties will agree in writing, before sharing, whether they act as controller, joint controller or processor, and will sign a data sharing or processing agreement where required.

7.3 Customer-level data is anonymised or aggregated before publication. Retention periods and access roles will follow the data plan approved by the Programme Management Office (PMO).

7.4 A party that suffers a personal data breach affecting Programme data will notify the PMO within \[48\] hours of becoming aware, so that notification duties to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner and Kenya Power can be met.

## 8. Cybersecurity (binding)

No Programme site will be connected remotely until it has passed the independent security audit required at Gate 3. Each party will follow the access control, patching and incident-response arrangements set by the Cybersecurity and Data Protection Officer, and will report suspected incidents to that officer without delay.

## 9. Intellectual property and open licensing (binding)

9.1 Each party keeps ownership of its pre-existing intellectual property.

9.2 Programme outputs (techno-economic model, risk register, training materials, reports, datasets and custom configuration or drivers) will be released under open licences. Software and repository content will use the **Apache License 2.0**, and documentation and data will use \[a Creative Commons licence, e.g. CC BY 4.0\], subject to Section 7 and Section 10.

9.3 OpenEMS components keep their own licences (Eclipse Public License 2.0 for Edge and Backend, AGPL 3.0 for the user interface). The Technical Lead keeps a licence compliance register, and the Partner will not remove or alter licence notices.

9.4 Where appropriate, custom drivers and fixes will be offered to the upstream OpenEMS community after Technical Working Group review.

## 10. Publication and independence (binding)

10.1 Results will be published openly after a Research and Evaluation Panel check for accuracy and a security and privacy review. Negative and inconclusive findings will be published with equal prominence.

10.2 The Research Lead reports evaluation results without editorial control by equipment suppliers or funders. The Partner may comment on drafts for \[14\] days but cannot require changes to findings.

10.3 A party may request a delay of up to \[60\] days to publication only to protect personal data, security or a legitimate legal interest.

## 11. Confidentiality (binding)

11.1 Information marked or reasonably understood as confidential will be used only for the Programme and not disclosed without the owner's consent, except as required by law, a court or a regulator.

11.2 This does not apply to information that is public, already known, independently developed, or lawfully received from a third party. Obligations continue for \[3\] years after this MoU ends.

## 12. Public statements

Parties may refer to the Programme and each other factually. Use of a party's name or logo in promotion needs its prior written consent. No party may suggest that regulatory approval or endorsement has been given unless it has.

## 13. Conflicts of interest and integrity

Each party will disclose any actual or potential conflict of interest (including supplier relationships) to the PMO, will not offer or accept improper inducements, and will comply with the **Bribery Act, 2016** and applicable anti-corruption laws.

## 14. Term, review and ending

14.1 This MoU takes effect on signature and lasts until \[the end of Programme closure at Gate 5, plus handover\], unless ended earlier.

14.2 The parties will review it at each stage gate and may amend it by written agreement.

14.3 Either party may end it on \[60\] days' written notice. Sections 7, 9, 10 and 11 survive ending.

## 15. Legal effect (binding)

15.1 Except for Sections 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 15 to 16, this MoU states intentions and is not legally binding. It creates no partnership, agency, employment or joint venture.

15.2 Each party's liability to the other is governed by any separate agreement. Nothing excludes liability that cannot lawfully be excluded.

## 16. Governing law and disputes (binding)

This MoU is governed by the laws of Kenya. The parties will first try to resolve disputes through the Steering Committee Chair within \[30\] days, then by mediation, and failing that by arbitration under the **Arbitration Act, 1995** administered by \[the Nairobi Centre for International Arbitration\], seated in Nairobi in English. Public bodies' immunities and statutory dispute processes are not waived except as the law allows.

# Part B: Host Site Agreement

**HOST SITE AGREEMENT**

**for the installation and operation of an OpenEMS pilot system at \[Site name\]**

**between**

**\[Programme Lead Entity\]**, of \[address\] (the "**Programme Lead**"),

**\[Host legal name\]**, of \[address, registration/ID no.\] (the "**Host**"),

**and, where the Host does not own the land or building,** **\[Landlord/Owner\]** (the "**Owner**"),

dated \[date\], for **Pilot \[A / B / C\]** (the "**Site**").

## 1. Definitions

"**Agreement**" means this agreement and its Schedules. "**Charter**" means the Programme Charter and Governance Plan. "**Equipment**" means all hardware and software installed under the Programme, including PV, batteries, inverters, meters, OpenEMS Edge device, EV chargers, networking and sensors, listed in Schedule 2. "**Pilot Period**" means the period from the Effective Date to the end of Programme closure at Gate 5 (about 24 months from programme start). "**Programme Data**" means all measurements and records generated by the Equipment. "**Utility**" means Kenya Power and Lighting Company PLC.

## 2. Purpose and priority

The Host agrees to host the Equipment at the Site so the Programme can measure how OpenEMS performs against a pre-agreed baseline. If this Agreement conflicts with a Schedule, the main body prevails, except where a Schedule expressly says otherwise.

## 3. Conditions precedent

The Programme Lead's obligations to procure and install are conditional on: (a) approval of the Site at Gate 1; (b) written consent of the Owner (if any); (c) Utility interconnection approval and any licence, permit or consent EPRA or county authorities require; (d) completion of compatibility testing and the security design review (Gate 2); and (e) an approved site safety plan. Either party may end this Agreement on \[30\] days' notice if a condition is not met by \[date\]. Neither is then liable to the other, except to return advances and each party's costs already incurred as set out in Schedule 5.

## 4. Access and site preparation

4.1 The Host grants the Programme Lead, its contractors, the university partner, the Utility and authorised evaluators a licence to access the Site on \[reasonable notice, 48 hours, except emergencies\] for installation, operation, inspection, data collection, training and removal.

4.2 The Host will provide the space, secure housing, power and (where agreed) connectivity set out in Schedule 1, and will not obstruct access.

4.3 The Host will give accurate information about the Site, its electrical installation and existing PV, loads and metering, and disclose known hazards.

## 5. Installation, commissioning and safety

5.1 Installation will be by competent, licensed contractors, following applicable Kenyan electrical standards, Utility requirements and the approved safety plan. Works that need a licensed electrical contractor or permit will be performed by one holding it.

5.2 Commissioning follows the standard checklist (electrical safety, control behaviour, offline operation, data logging, security). A commissioning certificate will be signed by the Site Lead and Host representative.

5.3 Each party will report safety incidents to the Site Lead within \[24\] hours. The Programme Lead reports incidents to the Steering Committee. Nobody other than authorised personnel may modify the Equipment or its configuration.

5.4 The Programme Lead will carry out risk assessments and fire-safety measures for battery systems proportionate to the Site, and will agree an emergency response plan with the Host and local responders.

## 6. Operation and control

6.1 OpenEMS controls the Equipment as set out in Schedule 3. The Host will not override controls except in an emergency or as the Schedule permits. Any override is logged.

6.2 **Baseline and test periods.** The Host agrees to the baseline and with/without-control test periods in the measurement and verification protocol, even if temporary operation may be less economical. The Programme Lead will compensate agreed unavoidable losses only as set in Schedule 5.

6.3 Edge devices must keep working during internet outages. The Host will inform the Site Lead if connectivity or power fails.

6.4 Remote connection starts only after Gate 3 (passed independent security audit). Before then, data may be collected locally.

## 7. Ownership of equipment

7.1 Unless Schedule 4 says otherwise, title to the Equipment remains with the \[Programme Lead / funder / named asset-holder\] during the Pilot Period. The Host will not sell, pledge, move or permit liens over it and will keep it free of charges.

7.2 The Equipment will not be treated as fixtures. If the Site is sold, leased or transferred, the Host will make the buyer or tenant bound by this Agreement or allow the Equipment's removal.

7.3 At closure, ownership or disposal of the Equipment follows Schedule 4: \[transfer to the Host at nil or agreed value / transfer to a named custodian / removal and decommissioning\]. Decommissioning costs are allocated in Schedule 4.

## 8. Costs and commercial terms

8.1 The Programme Lead bears the cost of supplying and installing the Equipment, as listed in Schedule 2. The Host bears the costs set out in Schedule 5 (for example site works, electricity for ordinary operations, and insurance of premises).

8.2 The Host keeps the benefit of any avoided energy costs or export credits, **\[or\]** shares them as set out in Schedule 5 *(select one)*. Net-metering applications, credits and tariff treatment remain between the Host and the Utility, and the Host remains responsible for its own electricity bills.

8.3 No fee is payable for OpenEMS software, which is released under open licences.

## 9. Data and privacy

9.1 The Programme Lead may collect Programme Data (energy flows, load profiles, tariffs, uptime, transformer loading, charger sessions, environmental data) to evaluate the Programme. Programme Data is shared with the university partner, the Utility (pilot feeder data) and, where approved, the Ministry of Energy and EPRA.

9.2 Programme Data is processed under the **Data Protection Act, 2019**. The Programme Lead and Host will record their roles in Schedule 6. For the EV site, the operator will display a privacy notice to charger users and will not collect driver identity or payment data in the Programme dataset unless a lawful basis and notice exist; any identifiers are pseudonymised.

9.3 Published results use aggregated or anonymised data. The Host's commercially sensitive information (for example production data or tariff contracts) will be identified in Schedule 6 and will not be published in identifiable form without consent. The Host may not prevent publication of aggregated results or findings.

9.4 The Host may access its own site data through the OpenEMS user interface. Access rights, roles and retention are in Schedule 6.

## 10. Cybersecurity

The Host will not connect Equipment to its internal network or other devices except as described in Schedule 3, will keep passwords and keys confidential, and will tell the Cybersecurity and Data Protection Officer of any suspected breach. Security patching and configuration changes follow the logged change process in the Charter.

## 11. Training and community

The Programme Lead will provide operator training for \[number\] Host staff or community operators and training materials. The Host will release trainees for training sessions and nominate a site representative. Community engagement, communications and a complaints channel follow Schedule 7 (in English and Kiswahili where relevant).

## 12. Intellectual property and open licensing

OpenEMS and the Programme outputs keep their open licences (Eclipse Public License 2.0, AGPL 3.0 for the user interface, Apache 2.0 for Programme software and configuration). The Host acquires no ownership of the Programme's software but receives the right to use the Equipment's software under those licences. The Host keeps its own IP and confidential business information.

## 13. Insurance

The Programme Lead will insure the Equipment (all risks including fire and theft) and carry public liability cover of at least KSh \[X\] during the Pilot Period. The Host will maintain property and public liability insurance for the premises with the Equipment noted. Certificates are exchanged before installation.

## 14. Risk, liability and indemnity

14.1 Risk of loss or damage to the Equipment lies with the party in control of it, except that the Host is responsible for damage caused by its negligence, misuse or breach (including unauthorised interference).

14.2 Each party is liable for death or personal injury caused by its negligence and for anything the law does not allow it to exclude.

14.3 Subject to 14.2, neither party is liable for indirect or consequential loss, including loss of profit or production, and the Programme Lead's aggregate liability to the Host is limited to KSh \[X\] or the value of the Equipment, whichever is \[lower/higher\]. The Programme is a research pilot and **no performance, savings or availability is guaranteed.**

14.4 Each party will indemnify the other for third-party claims to the extent arising from its negligence or breach.

## 15. Force majeure

Neither party is liable for delay or failure caused by events outside its reasonable control (including grid outages, severe weather, civil unrest, pandemic restrictions and regulatory action) if it notifies the other promptly and tries to limit the effect. If the event lasts over \[90\] days, either party may end this Agreement on notice.

## 16. Term and termination

16.1 This Agreement begins on \[date\] and continues until closure under Schedule 4, unless ended earlier.

16.2 Either party may end it for material breach not fixed within \[30\] days of written notice. The Programme Lead may end it for safety reasons immediately, for example unsafe conditions or unauthorised tampering. The Steering Committee may end a Site's participation after Gate review.

16.3 On ending, the Programme Lead will remove or make safe the Equipment as per Schedule 4, subject to the Host's obligation to give access. Data already collected may be kept and used for the evaluation under Section 9. Sections 7, 9, 12, 14, 17 and 18 survive.

## 17. Compliance and integrity

Each party will comply with applicable law (including the Energy Act, 2019, environmental and county requirements, occupational safety and health law, the Data Protection Act, 2019 and the Bribery Act, 2016) and will disclose conflicts of interest to the PMO. Environmental and social safeguards for battery handling, end-of-life and waste follow Schedule 7.

## 18. Governing law, disputes and general

18.1 Kenyan law governs this Agreement. Disputes go first to the Site Lead and Programme Manager, then to the Programme Director and the Host's senior representative, then to mediation, then to arbitration under the Arbitration Act, 1995 by \[the Nairobi Centre for International Arbitration\], seated in Nairobi, in English.

18.2 This Agreement and its Schedules are the whole agreement for the Site. Variations must be in writing and signed (Charter change control applies to material variations). Assignment needs consent. If a clause is invalid, the rest continues. Notices go to the addresses in Schedule 1. Counterparts and electronic signatures are valid.

## Points to decide before finalising

1. **Legal identity of the Programme Lead.** The Charter has no named contracting entity yet. All agreements need a legal person that can sign, hold insurance and (if applicable) hold equipment title.
2. **Equipment title.** Choose between Programme Lead, funder or host ownership, and the closure arrangement. This affects tax, insurance and warranties.
3. **Benefit sharing.** Decide whether hosts keep bill savings in full, which keeps the pilot simple and builds host buy-in, or share them.
4. **Financial thresholds.** Liability caps and procurement thresholds (KSh \[X\], \[Y\]) need funder confirmation.
5. **Licensing needs.** EPRA's written view on BESS, mini-grid and EV charging permits drives site conditions precedent.
6. **Dispute forum.** Confirm institution and public-body arbitration limits with counsel.
7. **Kiswahili versions.** Provide plain-language summaries for community-facing agreements, with the English version prevailing unless counsel advises otherwise.
