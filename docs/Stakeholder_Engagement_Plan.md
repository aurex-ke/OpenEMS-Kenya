# Stakeholder Engagement Plan

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This plan sets out how the programme will identify, analyse, engage and report back to everyone who affects or is affected by the pilot. It expands Section 22 of the Programme Charter and is owned by the Programme Management Office (PMO).

The programme can only succeed if regulators permit the pilot connections, Kenya Power provides grid access and data, host sites and communities accept the installations, and local people are trained to run them. Engagement is therefore treated as a delivery workstream with owners, deadlines and measures, not as communication added at the end.

The plan covers all stakeholders in the Charter (Section 9) across the 24-month pilot at Pilot A (industrial park or energy hub), Pilot B (rural microgrid) and Pilot C (EV charging site), and the period after closure when results are handed over.

## 2. Engagement Objectives

1. Secure regulatory clarity and approvals early, especially confirmation of net-metering terms and licensing for battery storage and EV charging.
2. Obtain Kenya Power's agreement on interconnection, data access and grid-engineer participation before design is fixed.
3. Win informed consent and lasting acceptance from host site owners and communities.
4. Build local OpenEMS capacity through universities, technicians and operators.
5. Keep funders and the Steering Committee informed with accurate, evidence-based reporting, including bad news.
6. Make findings useful to regulators, utilities and developers by involving them in shaping the evaluation and publishing results openly.

## 3. Engagement Principles

- **Early and continuous.** Regulators, utilities and communities are engaged before design decisions, consistent with the Charter's governance principles.
- **Honest and evidence-first.** We state what is known and unknown. International savings of 15–30% are hypotheses, not promises, and community messaging must not overpromise tariff reductions, jobs or supply improvements.
- **Two-way.** Every engagement activity records input received and what was done with it.
- **Inclusive.** Women, youth, people with disabilities, small businesses and low-income households are deliberately reached, not only leaders and large customers.
- **Accessible.** Materials are in plain language, in English and Kiswahili where relevant, and use formats suited to the audience (meetings, radio, posters, short briefings).
- **Open by default.** Methods and findings are shared publicly, with privacy and security safeguards.
- **Proportionate.** Effort is matched to each group's influence, interest and exposure to risk.

## 6. Engagement Strategy by Stakeholder Group

### 6.1 Ministry of Energy and EPRA

- **Interests and concerns:** Evidence for national policy; safe, compliant deployment; no pre-judging of tariff or regulatory decisions (changes to tariffs and regulations are out of the programme's scope).
- **What we need:** Confirmation of net-metering terms; guidance on licensing of battery storage and EV charging; EPRA observer on the Research and Evaluation Panel; Ministry or funder chair for the Steering Committee; possible hosting of the Backend.
- **Approach:** Early bilateral briefings before the feasibility study; membership of the SteerCo and Regulatory and Stakeholder Forum; a standing regulatory issues log; sensitivity tests on lower export credits shared with EPRA.
- **Frequency:** Quarterly at SteerCo, twice yearly at the Forum, plus ad hoc meetings at triggers such as any formal proposal to change net-metering or licensing.
- **Owner:** Programme Director.

### 6.2 Kenya Power and Local Utilities

- **Interests and concerns:** Grid safety and stability, data security, impact on network operations and revenue, workload on staff.
- **What we need:** Interconnection approvals, access to feeder and transformer data, a grid engineer on the Technical Working Group, notification routes for incidents.
- **Approach:** Joint technical workshops during site selection; agreed data-sharing protocol; shared incident response plan; invitation to attend commissioning and review sessions; early engagement to avoid the "approval not secured by Gate 1" risk.
- **Frequency:** Fortnightly through the TWG during build, monthly in operation; quarterly at SteerCo.
- **Owner:** Technical Lead, with the Programme Director for approvals.

### 6.3 Funders

- **Interests and concerns:** Value for money, risk control, visible results, compliance with funding agreements.
- **Approach:** Monthly dashboard, quarterly SteerCo reporting, early notice of overruns or delays, annual audit access, joint review at each gate. Negative or inconclusive findings are reported with equal prominence.
- **Owner:** Programme Director and finance lead.

### 6.4 Universities and Training Institutions

- **Interests and concerns:** Research access, publication rights, student involvement, independence.
- **Approach:** Formal partnership agreement covering data access, authorship and independence; university lead on the Research and Evaluation Panel; student projects and internships on measurement and OpenEMS configuration; joint training workshops.
- **Frequency:** Monthly in operation through the Panel; per-semester planning.
- **Owner:** Research Lead.

### 6.5 Technology Partners, Vendors and the OpenEMS Community

- **Interests and concerns:** Commercial opportunity, clear specifications, fair procurement, warranty and service obligations; for the OpenEMS community, quality contributions and licence compliance.
- **Approach:** Pre-procurement market briefing; compatibility testing before award; written service-level expectations; no editorial control over evaluation by vendors; custom drivers developed in the open and offered upstream where appropriate, with licence compliance tracked.
- **Frequency:** At procurement milestones and fortnightly during build.
- **Owner:** Technical Lead and procurement lead.

### 6.6 Host Site Owners and Managers

- **Interests and concerns:** Disruption to operations, safety, liability, ownership of equipment, bills and tariffs, data confidentiality, reputation.
- **Approach:** Written host agreements before procurement covering access, safety, asset ownership and end-of-pilot arrangements, and data use; a named host representative on each site team; weekly site operations check-in; advance notice of outages and works.
- **Owner:** Site Leads, with the Programme Manager for agreements.

### 6.7 Communities and End Users

Communities hold the least formal power but carry the most direct exposure, so engagement here is designed most carefully. Section 7 gives the detail.

### 6.8 County Governments, Civil Society and Media

- **Approach:** County briefings at site selection for permits and local coordination; a short public fact sheet and a project web page in the open repository; media briefings at commissioning and at publication of results; invitation to the Regulatory and Stakeholder Forum for relevant civil society groups.
- **Owner:** Programme Manager.

## 7. Community and Site-Level Engagement

### 7.1 Before site selection

- Consult candidate host communities and site owners before any site is chosen, as set out in the Charter, and record their views in the site selection evidence for Gate 1.
- Use local leaders and trusted institutions (for example administrators, cooperatives, schools, clinics, faith and women's groups) to reach a wide cross-section, but also hold separate sessions for women, youth and small business owners so quieter voices are heard.
- Obtain documented informed agreement from the host community for Pilot B before procurement. Agreement means people understand what will be installed, what is uncertain and what they can expect to change, and can say no.

### 7.2 Messages that must be clear and honest

- This is a **research pilot**: results, savings and reliability gains are being measured, not guaranteed.
- What the equipment is, where it will be, who owns and operates it, and what happens at the end of the pilot.
- Any effect on tariffs, billing or supply during the pilot (to be confirmed with Kenya Power and the host agreements).
- How data is collected, who sees it, and how customer-level data is anonymised or aggregated before publication.
- How to ask questions or complain, and what will be done with feedback.

### 7.3 During build and operation

- Plain-language updates at least quarterly, in English and Kiswahili where relevant, delivered through community meetings, notice boards and local radio or messaging channels as appropriate.
- Notice of construction, expected disruption and safety precautions before works begin, with attention to children, livestock and traffic near the site.
- Operator and community training sessions, tracked by number trained, assessed competence and retention.
- For Pilot C, briefings for drivers and fleet operators on how managed charging works, expected effects on charging times, and how to give feedback. Charging delays are measured and reported, not hidden.
- For Pilot A, briefings for staff and neighbouring businesses on works, safety and expected operating changes.

### 7.4 Local benefit and capacity

- Prefer local labour and services where qualified and cost-effective, and record local hiring.
- Train local technicians and operators so the community or host can operate and support the system without continuous foreign support, which is a programme-level success criterion.
- Do not promise employment, free electricity or permanent assets beyond what host agreements state.

## 11. Feedback and Grievance Mechanism

- **Channels:** At least two ways to raise a concern at each site, for example a named community liaison, a phone or messaging line, a suggestion box and the site lead. Anonymous submission is allowed.
- **Process:** Acknowledge within \[3\] working days; assign an owner; propose a response within \[14\] days; tell the person what was decided; record in the complaints log. Thresholds are proposed and for approval.
- **Escalation:** Unresolved or serious issues go to the Programme Manager, then the Programme Director, then the Steering Committee. Safety and security concerns are escalated immediately under the incident response plan.
- **Review:** The PMO reviews the log monthly, reports themes to the SteerCo quarterly and uses them to adjust activities.
- **Non-retaliation:** People raising concerns are not penalised, and personal details are kept confidential.

## 12. Engagement Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulators or Kenya Power slow to confirm terms or approvals | Early engagement; named counterparts; shortlist of backup sites; escalate through Sponsor and SteerCo | Programme Director |
| Community expectations exceed what a pilot can deliver | Clear research-pilot messaging; written expectations in host agreements; regular honest updates | Site Leads |
| Concerns about tariffs, bills or fairness | Confirm any billing effects in advance with Kenya Power; plain-language explanations; grievance channel | Programme Director |
| Community or host opposition, including from groups not consulted | Broad consultation including women, youth and small businesses; separate sessions; right to decline; backup sites | Site Leads |
| Perceived bias by vendors or funders in results | Independent Research Panel; no editorial control by suppliers or funders; publish negative findings equally | Research Lead |
| Data or security incident damages trust | Security audit before go-live; incident response plan with notification routes to Kenya Power and, where required, EPRA | Cybersecurity Officer |
| Stakeholder fatigue or overlapping requests | Single coordinated calendar; combine meetings where possible; share outputs back | Programme Manager |
| Loss of trained local staff | Retention tracking, refresher training, documented procedures, custodians named at closure | Technical Lead |
| Poor connectivity limits communication in rural areas | Offline-friendly materials, in-person sessions, local liaison | Site Lead (Pilot B) |

## 13. Monitoring and Evaluation of Engagement

Engagement performance is reported on the monthly dashboard and to the SteerCo quarterly. Targets are to be set at Gate 1.

| Indicator | Measure |
| --- | --- |
| Regulatory and utility agreements | Net-metering clarity and interconnection approvals secured by Gate 1 |
| Participation | Number and diversity of people reached, including share of women and youth, at each site |
| Stakeholder confidence | Short pulse survey or interviews at Gates 1, 3 and 5; trend in acceptance at host sites |
| Responsiveness | Share of complaints acknowledged and resolved within agreed times |
| Information delivery | Quarterly updates delivered on time; materials available in required languages |
| Capacity | Operators and technicians trained, assessed as competent and retained (Charter KPI) |
| Uptake of results | Number of regulators, utilities or developers adopting the techno-economic model or risk register |
| Open publication | Findings, datasets and code published as planned |

The plan is reviewed at each gate and updated when the stakeholder register changes materially, for example when a site changes or a new regulator proposal emerges.

## 14. Resources and Budget

Engagement costs are drawn from the programme budget rather than a separate line, mainly from training and capacity building (about 5% of the reference cost structure) and the PMO. Cost items to be estimated during feasibility include community liaison officers, translation and printing, meeting costs, radio or messaging channels, workshops and events, and the complaints channel. Amount: KSh \[to be confirmed at Gate 1\]. Any use of contingency for engagement requires SteerCo approval.

## 15. Disclosure and Publication

- Results are published openly after a Research Panel accuracy check and a security and privacy review.
- Customer-level data is anonymised or aggregated before publication, in line with Kenyan data protection law and host agreements.
- Stakeholders directly involved (host sites, Kenya Power, EPRA, communities) receive findings before public release so there are no surprises.
- Negative or inconclusive findings are published with equal prominence.
